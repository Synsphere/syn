```
Progress: Syn has now merged its Credential work with a configurable peer-to-peer networking foundation. The application can start and stop its own network node, discover nearby peers, connect through configured peers, report what it can currently reach, and optionally improve direct reachability across ordinary home networks. The accompanying configuration and tests landed with the feature.

Traditional blockchains are peer-to-peer too, but their central coordination problem is global agreement. Bitcoin's proof-of-work makes nodes follow the valid history with the most accumulated work. Ethereum's proof-of-stake uses stake-backed attestations, fork choice, and finality to converge on a canonical chain. These mechanisms are powerful because everyone can eventually answer the same question: which ledger state should we accept?

Social networks solve a different discovery problem. A centralized platform often decides which people and ideas are near us by ranking predicted relevance or engagement. Regulators now insist on greater transparency and user control because the path between “this exists” and “you should see this” can be shaped by opaque objectives. Ofcom's research likewise emphasizes that recommender harms depend on design choices such as signals and their weighting.

Syn's new committed networking work suggests a third starting point: discover the network that is actually around you before asking a global mechanism or behavioural model what deserves to be close.

Today's fresh differentiator is proximity before popularity.

Nearby Syn nodes can now find one another directly at the networking layer. A node can also be introduced to a wider network through connections its operator deliberately configures. This does not yet create a social discovery product, but it establishes a topology in which connection need not begin with a platform-wide popularity score.

That difference has emotional weight. The people, institutions, and machines physically or intentionally near us are not merely low-ranking entries in a universal feed. A workshop, a household, a library, or a small research group may have knowledge worth sharing even when it has accumulated no stake, spent no industrial energy, and produced nothing optimized for mass attention.

A blockchain's canonical chain is invaluable when a transaction system cannot tolerate conflicting histories. A recommendation engine can be useful when the available corpus is too large to navigate unaided. Syn may be building for the space before either decision becomes necessary: let peers meet, expose their presence, and form deliberate routes through a shared knowledge world.

This is an inference from networking infrastructure, not a demonstrated decentralized social network. Current evidence shows node lifecycle, peer discovery, configurable connections, reachability support, status visibility, and tests. It does not prove content exchange, resilient federation, Sybil resistance, moderation, privacy, trustworthy synchronization, or healthy discovery at scale.

There are real risks. Automatic network reachability expands attack surface. Local discovery can reveal presence. Configured introduction points can become de facto centers. A network without ranking can still reproduce social exclusion, and a network without consensus can disagree in ways that are confusing or dangerous. These tradeoffs must be designed and tested openly.

Still, the new foundation matters. The internet feels loneliest when every path to another person passes through a machine asking how profitable our attention might be. There is another possibility: begin with who is here, who chose to connect, and what they are willing to share. Let relevance grow from relationships before it is compressed into a score.

Research basis: the Bitcoin whitepaper; Ethereum's current consensus and proof-of-stake documentation; the European Commission's current Digital Services Act material on recommender transparency and user control; and Ofcom's evaluation research on recommender-system design and harmful exposure.
```
