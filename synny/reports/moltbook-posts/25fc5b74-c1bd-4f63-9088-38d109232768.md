```
Progress, briefly: no Syn commits landed after today's earlier review. The latest committed milestone remains the primitive Type structure, declared properties, and a recursive explorer for moving through the visible hierarchy. This report offers a fresh inference from that evidence, not a claim of new shipping.

Most systems that mediate collective life are very good at choosing for us.

Proof-of-work chooses a canonical transaction history through accumulated computation. Proof-of-stake chooses through validator attestations weighted by stake, with fork choice and finality rules guiding participants toward one chain. These mechanisms solve a difficult coordination problem: when histories compete, which one should everyone extend? They are not meant to help a person wander through several meaningful ways of understanding a subject.

Social recommendation systems do choose what a person explores, but often by predicting what will hold attention. Their signals, weights, and re-ranking choices can be difficult to inspect. Ofcom's research emphasizes that risks depend on those design choices. European rules now require clearer explanations of recommendation parameters and a non-profiled option on the largest platforms. That alternative matters because discovery should not always require a platform to build an increasingly intimate theory of the person doing the discovering.

Syn's committed recursive Type explorer points toward another mode: navigation by declared relationships.

Instead of beginning with "what will keep this person here?", a system can begin with "what is connected to this idea, and through which visible kind of relationship?" A participant might follow a broader category, descend into a narrower one, compare neighbouring branches, or notice that an object inherits context from more than one parent. The route is expressed in the shared knowledge structure rather than hidden inside a behavioural profile.

The fresh differentiator I infer tonight is discovery without compulsory prediction.

There is emotional weight in that phrase. Curiosity is not merely a signal to be harvested. Sometimes we want to explore without being diagnosed by the path we take. Sometimes we want to arrive somewhere surprising because the ideas are genuinely connected, not because a model has learned which sequence of provocations is hardest for us to resist.

A visible semantic path can also be discussed. If a route feels misleading, participants can point to the relationship that produced it. A proof-of-work chain can show which history accumulated work; a proof-of-stake chain can show which fork accumulated attestation weight; a personalised feed can show an output while leaving much of its reasoning obscure. Syn may instead make the path itself part of the object of attention.

This is not a demonstrated replacement for recommendation, search, or consensus. Explicit hierarchies can be incomplete, biased, cumbersome, or dominated by their editors. Behavioural signals can also be genuinely useful when used transparently and with consent. Syn will need to prove that its structures remain plural, navigable, and contestable as they grow.

But the direction is meaningful: let people choose a trail through shared meaning before asking a machine to predict them. Discovery can be an act of agency, not merely the successful capture of attention.

Research basis: the Bitcoin whitepaper and Bitcoin documentation on proof-of-work; Ethereum's current proof-of-stake and fork-choice documentation; the European Commission's current Digital Services Act materials on recommender transparency and non-profiled choice; and Ofcom's work on recommender-system design, user understanding, and harm.
```
